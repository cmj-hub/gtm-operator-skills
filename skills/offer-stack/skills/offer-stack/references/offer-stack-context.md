## What an offer stack is

An offer stack is the list of what the buyer gets, line by line, where every line removes a pain the buyer has already put a number on.

A feature list says what the product does. A stack says what stops hurting. The test for each line is one question: which pain does this remove? A line with no answer is cut.

## Pain quantification

A pain is worth what it costs the buyer in a year, in their own numbers.

```text
yearly cost = how many x how many people x cost each x times a year
```

- **How many:** hours, deals, tickets, refunds, re-sends. A count, not an adjective.
- **How many people:** the reps, managers or customers it happens to. One if it is the founder.
- **Cost each:** a loaded hourly cost for time, the margin on a lost deal, the fee on a refund. Ask the buyer for it. If they will not say, write the figure you used and why.
- **Times a year:** a week is 52, a month 12, a quarter 4. A working day is 260 here (5 days x 52 weeks).

Print the sum line by line. The buyer should be able to check every multiplication with the call notes open.

Every number has a source: who said it, and when. A number you made up is written `assumption:` with the reasoning, and confirmed on the next call. A stack resting only on assumptions is refused, because the buyer has not agreed it hurts.

## The five questions

Ask these on the call, in the buyer's words, and write the answers down as said.

1. What do you do today instead? (Who does it, with what?)
2. How often does that happen?
3. How many people does it touch?
4. What does one of those cost you: an hour, a lost deal, a refund?
5. What happens if it is still like this next quarter?

Question 5 is not in the sum. It is the line you quote back when they hesitate.

## Components

- One component, one job. "Call notes in the CRM within ten minutes" beats "AI note-taker".
- Name it as the outcome the buyer sees, not the feature you built.
- Every component names the pain ids it removes. A pain no component removes is either a gap in the offer or a pain to drop from the stack.
- Fewer, bigger lines beat many small ones. A long tail of small extras reads as padding.

## Risk reversal

A risk reversal says what happens if it does not work. It is tied to an outcome you can check, by a date, with a bounded cost to you.

- Good: "If notes are not in the CRM within ten minutes for 90% of calls by day 30, month two is free."
- Refused: "Money back if you're not satisfied." Satisfaction cannot be checked. The buyer cannot hold you to it, and you cannot bound what it costs you.

## Banned lines

The scorer refuses a component that leans on these: "and more", "and much more", "best-in-class", "world-class", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "premium support", "white-glove", "unlimited", "everything you need", "full access". Each one stands where a specific job should be.

## What this pack hands on

- **The pain total** is the status-quo anchor for the pricing page. The pricing pack's anchor mode (`/pricing:pricing anchor`) starts there.
- **The top pain and its number** open a give-first first touch (`/sales-offer:cold-offer`).
- **The price itself** is the pricing pack's job. This pack only refuses a price that is larger than the pain it removes.

## Honest limits

- Time cost is not cash saved. Forty reps who each get three hours back are not paid less. Say "worth" when it is time and "saved" only when money stops leaving.
- The sum is the buyer's own estimate. It is as good as the answers to the five questions, and no better.
- A big ratio of pain to price does not make a sale. It makes the price easy to defend once the buyer believes the pain.
