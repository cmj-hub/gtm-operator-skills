# Interview

One question per message. Before each, check the fact table. If a source already answers it, skip, and say what you used.

## Contents

- Opening
- Questions
- Skip rules
- When the user does not know

## Opening

Say: "I will ask a few short questions, one at a time, and skip anything your site or docs already cover. Give me a website or any docs first, or say skip."

If `$ARGUMENTS` already holds a source, say only the first sentence and name the source you are reading.

## Questions

Ask in this order. The bracket names the file the answer fills.

1. In one or two sentences, what do you sell? [company]
2. Who is the buyer, and what kind of company are they at? [company, icp]
3. How do you charge? Per seat, per project, a monthly fee, usage? Words are fine. [company]
4. How big is the team, and how long have you been selling? [company]
5. Which customer segments are best for you? Name one to three. [icp]
6. For each segment: size, industry, region, and tools they already run. [icp]
7. What happens in a buyer's world that makes them look for you now? Things someone could observe, such as a hire, a launch, or a missed target. [icp]
8. Who feels the problem, and who signs? [icp]
9. Who should we never write to, and why? [icp]
10. What are your offers, and what is in each? Include any trial, pilot, or free first step that is not on your site. [offer]
11. What changes for a buyer after they use you? In their words. [offer]
12. What is the first step a buyer takes? [offer]
13. Which results can you cite? For each: what happened and where it is written down. [proof]
14. Any customer quotes you have permission to use? [proof]
15. Describe how you want to sound in three words, then name words you use and words you never use. [voice]
16. Paste two or three things you wrote that sound like you. I will pick sample lines from them. [voice]
17. What do buyers say before they say yes? Their words, up to five. [objections]
17b. Pick the three that come up most. For each, one at a time: what do you say back? [objections]
18. What do buyers do today instead of buying from you, and why do they pick it? Kinds of things, not company names. [categories]

## Skip rules

- A fact from a website or doc counts as answered when it is stated plainly and in full. Record its URL or path.
- A source that answers only part counts for the part. Say what you used and ask for what is missing. A site rarely lists every offer, so for question 10 always ask what the site leaves out.
- When the user and a source disagree, the user wins. Keep the source's version as a `Source differs:` line.
- A fact you inferred does not count. Ask, and offer your guess: "I read X from your site. Is that right?"
- A source never answers questions 7, 9, 13 (the source of a result), 15, 17, or 17b. Ask those.
- A result found on a site goes in proof.md with that page as its Source, and the claim copied as written.
- If the user answers a later question early, record it and skip that question when you reach it.
- After each file's questions, show the two-line summary and ask "Anything to add or fix?", including for a file where every question was skipped.

## When the user does not know

Write `Unknown:` and what is missing, then move on. For a result with no source, write it with `Source: unverified`. Do not push.
